Order Is Non-Negotiable

5-Step Opening Sequence

1
0–15 min · Role Brief
Finance Officer writes margin target and credit limit on paper. Analyst starts the canvas. No negotiations begin until these are recorded.
2
15–22 min · Call BOTH Destinations
"What volume do you need? What is your ceiling CIF Rotterdam?" Write the answer down. This is your ceiling. Never commit to a buy price before this step.
3
22–25 min · Finance Officer Calculates Max Buy
Formula: Max buy = Ceiling − Freight − Target margin. Example: $240 − $22 − $8 = $210/t FOB. This is the number you walk into the originator call with.
4
25–38 min · Call Originators
Armed with ceiling and max buy price. "We are looking at X tonnes. Where can you come in?" If their ask exceeds max buy: counter or walk.
5
38–44 min · Lock Both Sides Simultaneously
Sign both term sheets within 60 seconds of each other. Do not sign the buy-side before verbal confirmation from the sell side. One-legged deals are the most expensive mistake in the simulation.

Never Do This

Five Deadly Errors

  • ✕
    Call the Originator before you know the Destination's ceiling. You will commit blind.
  • ✕
    Sign a buy-side term sheet before verbal confirmation from the sell side.
  • ✕
    Exceed credit limit of EUR 1,050,000,000. Finance Officer tracks this on every deal.
  • ✕
    Accept a margin below zero — even under time pressure. Walk and find a different deal.
  • ✕
    Lock a CIF sell price without simultaneously locking freight. A Tension Card can spike freight post-deal.

Blending Opportunity — Look For This

If GrainPath has excess feed wheat at $195/t FOB and EuroMill needs milling grade at $240/t CIF:

Buy $195 + $9 blending + $22 freight = $226 effective CIF
Sell at $237/t CIF = EUR 11/t margin on a lower-cost input.

Neither Originator nor Destination can access this margin. It is NovaCargo's exclusive advantage — use it every turn.

Phase A · Minutes 15–22

Call Destination First

You →
"EuroMill — we are in the market for milling wheat this turn. What volume do you need and what is your ceiling price CIF Rotterdam?"
EuroMill →
"We need 35,000 tonnes. We can go to $238/t CIF if the spec is right."
Finance Officer Note →
Ceiling $238 CIF. Max buy = $238 − $22 freight − $8 margin = $208/t FOB. This is the number.

Phase B · Minutes 25–38

Call Originator Second

You →

GrainPath →
"We are at $218/t."

"That is above where we can work. We have a buyer at $208/t net. If you can come to $207, we can lock it immediately — both sides today."
GrainPath →
"$210 — final."
Finance Check →
$210 buy + $22 freight = $232 CIF cost. Sell $238. Margin = $6/t. Below EUR 8 target but above floor. Proceed if volume compensates.
You →
"Deal at $210. Contract Manager — prepare both term sheets simultaneously. We sign with GrainPath and EuroMill in the next 10 minutes."

Pre-Signing Checklist

4 Mandatory Clauses

  • 1
    Clause 1 — Commodity & Grade
    "Milling Wheat, minimum 11.5% protein, maximum 14% moisture, origin Ukraine, SGS analysis at discharge port Rotterdam." Never write just "Wheat."
  • 2
    Clause 2 — Price & Delivery Basis
    Price per tonne AND delivery basis (FOB or CIF). Without the basis, no one knows who pays the $22/t freight. On sell-side always offer CIF. On buy-side accept FOB.
  • 3
    Clause 3 — Volume & Payment
    "35,000 tonnes ±3% · Payment: 75 days sight LC from Bill of Lading date." Match buy and sell payment terms — mismatch means you fund the gap.
  • 4
    Clause 4 — Force Majeure
    Must name: port strike, government export ban, Bosphorus corridor restriction, natural disaster at origin. Buy-side and sell-side FM clauses must mirror each other — if one side invokes FM and yours doesn't cover it, you are exposed.
